The idea in one paragraph

Most "free" games on your phone really mean ad-supported, tracked, and pay-to-win. Free open-source games are different in kind: their source code is public under a license that guarantees you can keep playing, copying, and sharing them forever. No company can add ads to them next month. No server shutdown can take them away from you. This guide shows you how to find your favorites, get them installed, plug in a controller, and give good ones as gifts — all in plain English, step by step.

Install tips (plain English)

Rule number one: download from the game's official site (the "Official site" link in our directory), or from your device's normal app store (Google Play, Apple App Store, F-Droid, Steam, or your Linux package manager). Skip random download-mirror sites — several of them wrap free games in installers full of adware.

Where you playThe easy path
WindowsOfficial site installer, or itch.io / Steam (several FLOSS games are there, price $0). Versions on GitHub tag pages are trustworthy for most of these projects.
macOSSame official site; you may need to right-click → Open the first time because the app isn't from the official Launchpad store.
iPhone/iPadLook on the App Store by exact title, e.g. Shattered Pixel Dungeon. (Note: iOS's strict sandbox rules mean not every FLOSS game ships there.)
AndroidGoogle Play or, even better for privacy, F-Droid — a store that exclusively carries open-source apps, and is itself open source.
LinuxYour distro's package manager (Flatpak or Snap often has the newest versions), or the project's own download page.
Browser2048 and Hextris play in a tab with zero installs, and Dungeon Crawl Stone Soup offers official online play straight from its website.

Two habits keep you safe: (1) check that the browser bar shows the site from the directory before you click a Download button, and (2) when a game offers an "official launcher," that launcher (such as Veloren's Airshipper) usually stays updated forever, so consider it.

Practical tips: any cheap USB gamepad works on Windows, Linux, and most of these games out of the box; Xbox-style controllers have the broadest built-in support. On macOS, wired/wireless Xbox controllers connect over Bluetooth in System Settings. For the most keyboard-heavy games, a $10 keyboard is a better gift than a fancy controller.

Gift-able picks: give great games for $0

"Free forever" licenses mean you can legally copy and share these games — a genuinely free gift, no gray areas. Ideal when you want to give something fun without spending: birthdays, students, distant family, friends with no safe download habits.

  • For phone people: Shattered Pixel Dungeon — free on both big app stores, installed in 30 seconds, a lovely gift link.
  • For families: SuperTuxKart — four-player split-screen with controllers is a legitimate game-night replacement; hand everyone a USB gamepad.
  • For strategy souls: The Battle for Wesnoth and OpenTTD — hundreds of hours, zero dollars, easy to talk them into installing.
  • For browser-only comfort: 2048 — send the official link of the original (no-clone, no ads) version.
  • For the person who has everything: Veloren with its friendly launcher — "it updates itself; you just press Play."

A gentle, legal upgrade you can offer: projects like SuperTuxKart, Wesnoth, and Veloren accept donations to their developers, and Mindustry's Steam edition is the "pay what feels right" copy. Gifts that support the humans who made the game — never required, sometimes right.

FLOSS licenses explained in 90 seconds

"FLOSS" = Free/Libre and Open-Source Software. The license is what makes a game free forever rather than merely free this week. You don't need to read legal text — a few plain families cover almost every game in our directory:

License familyIn plain EnglishThe catchDirectory examples
GPL (v2, v3, "v2-or-later") Free to play, copy, share, and modify — but if you hand someone a modified copy, you must also share your new source code under the GPL. "Share-alike": your modified version stays open. That's the point. 0 A.D., SuperTuxKart, Xonotic, Veloren, Endless Sky, Mindustry
LGPL GPL's softer cousin, mostly used for building-block libraries rather than whole games. Same spirit, gentler rules. Luanti (formerly Minetest)
MIT / BSD Free to do nearly anything with the code (keep the copyright notice). The most permissive family. Someone could build a closed version. Doesn't affect you, just know it. 2048, Freedoom
Project-specific open licenses Custom licenses that still sail through the Open Source Initiative's check (e.g., NetHack's own GPL-style license). Read the game's docs if you plan to modify and redistribute. NetHack (NGPL)
CC licenses (art & audio) Frequently paired with the code license: the game's art and sound are separately licensed, often Creative Commons. Applies to media, not the program. Common across all FLOSS games

The one-sentence takeaway: copyleft licenses (GPL family) say "free forever and your improvements stay free too"; permissive licenses (MIT/BSD) say "free forever, do what you like." Both are legit; both mean the game can never suddenly demand a monthly fee.

Verified links — how we check them

Every external link in this guide and in the games directory was programmatically checked at publication (HTTP status checked with an automated script; the methodology is in our repository's README). We only link to official project websites and their public source repositories — never third-party mirrors, never download portals, never affiliate links.

If a link ever rots, please report it. Updating is a one-line fix; these files are plain HTML/CSS with no scripts, no analytics, and no external assets, so the whole thing works offline from your own computer.
